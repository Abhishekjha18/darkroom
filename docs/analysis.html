01

How the scoring works

The published rubric is four criteria on a 5-point scale: Functionality & Usefulness 35%, Zero-Dependency Craft 30%, Code Quality & Idiom 25%, Innovation 10%. That weighted average is the Core column, and it is the only number the judges actually compute.

Core alone is a bad decision tool, because it prices a project you finish identically to one you don't. Four multipliers correct for that:

  • Ship confidence — probability of landing a strong, complete artifact solo in 72 hours. Applied directly: an unfinished submission scores near zero on the 35% criterion regardless of how good the idea was.
  • Demo strength — revised upward in this pass. The five-minute video is a required deliverable and it is where a judge forms their impression of Functionality before reading any code. Weighting it as a tiebreaker was wrong. ±10%.
  • Depth — reward for more implementation. Estimated substantive lines, banded, ±8%. Deliberately gentle: volume is evidence of craft, not a judged criterion, and past a point it converts into risk rather than score.
  • Crowding — how many other entrants build something recognisably similar. Being the fortieth hand-rolled parser costs real Innovation points; being the only photo tool earns them. ±5%.

Index = (0.35·U + 0.30·C + 0.25·Q + 0.10·I) × Ship × Demo × Depth × Crowding

On false precision. These are judgments encoded as numbers, not measurements. Two decimals make the ranking legible, not authoritative — treat anything inside ±0.15 as a tie and decide on taste.

03

darkroom versus zdrop

These two finish 0.02 apart, which is a tie. They share a demo moment — the QR code, the phone, the LAN — because darkroom absorbs zdrop's best trick for about 400 lines of code. The QR encodes http://192.168.x.x:8080; no service discovery protocol is required at all, so the fusion costs you a Reed–Solomon encoder and a mask-selection pass, and nothing else.

What separates them is everything behind the QR code.

04

The five minutes, storyboarded

Since the demo is the deciding artifact, design it first and build backwards from it. This is the running order, and every rung of the build ladder below exists to serve a beat in it.

  1. 0:00 — The manifest, first. cat Cargo.toml. Eleven lines, [dependencies] empty. Then cargo tree, showing one node. Get the receipt out of the way in twenty seconds so the rest of the video is product.
  2. 0:20 — One command. cargo build --release, then darkroom ~/Pictures. A QR code draws itself in the terminal.
  3. 0:50 — The phone. Physically pick up a phone, scan the code on screen, and let the gallery load on the handset. No app, no login, no internet. This is the beat people will remember.
  4. 1:40 — The payload. "You have 1,300 duplicate photos and 400 near-duplicates." Open the review view, show two visually identical shots at different resolutions sitting side by side, and reclaim four gigabytes on camera.
  5. 2:40 — Lift the lid. Show the JPEG decoder, the IDCT, the perceptual hash. State plainly that the browser is not decoding these thumbnails — you are. This is where the 30% Craft score is won.
  6. 3:40 — The receipts. Build it twice, diff the two SHA-256 hashes, show them identical. Scroll STDLIB.md past twelve substitutions.
  7. 4:30 — Honest limits. Baseline JPEG only, no progressive. No HEIC. Naive resampling. Say it out loud — the rules explicitly reward disclosed shortcomings over concealed ones, and volunteering them reads as confidence.
05

Why it survives contact with the clock

80% ship confidence on a 5,500-line project rests on one structural fact: a working, submittable artifact exists at every rung. You never trade "finished but small" against "ambitious but broken" — you climb until the clock stops and ship the rung you reached. Note what rung one does not require: any image decoding at all, because the browser decodes originals perfectly well on its own.

  1. Folder walk, HTTP server, browser gridServe originals straight to the browser, CSS-sized. Concurrent connections via a thread pool. You now have a submittable tool and zero image code has been written.
  2. Baseline JPEG decode → PNG thumbnailsHuffman tables, IDCT, YCbCr conversion, box downsampling, then PNG encode via DEFLATE and CRC32. On-disk thumbnail cache. The library becomes fast, and the Craft score arrives.
  3. EXIF / TIFF IFD parsingCapture date, orientation, camera model, GPS. Photos rotate correctly and group into a timeline. Cheap work, disproportionate polish.
  4. Perceptual hashing and clusteringdHash for speed, DCT-based pHash for accuracy, Hamming-distance clustering, side-by-side review, safe delete with a trash directory. This is the emotional payload of the demo.
  5. QR generationReed–Solomon error correction, mask selection, terminal block rendering. The phone beat goes live. Self-contained, well-specified, no protocol risk.
  6. PolishLanczos resampling, upload-from-phone, keyboard navigation, whatever the clock allows.
  7. The paperwork is not optionalREADME with honest limits, STDLIB.md at twelve-plus substitutions, deps-proof.txt, .zero-dep.toml, reproducible build hashes, and the video. Ten hours, reserved, not borrowed against.

The bonus challenges

"Pick one and nail it" is advice against spreading thin, not a limit on claims. Three of four are nearly free on this project. Only one demands a real sacrifice.

Reproducible Build+5~3 hoursClaim Highest points-per-hour item available, and idea-independent — every Rust candidate can claim it equally, so it never affects which project you pick. Pin the toolchain, pass --remap-path-prefix, disable incremental, build twice, publish both hashes, and document the envelope honestly: same machine, same toolchain version.

Package Killer+3~1 hourClaim sharp at ~7M weekly downloads is the headline, with qrcode at ~3M behind it. Weaker than anyfmt's js-yaml claim, stronger than most.
Single File+5StructuralSkip Opposes the instruction to maximise implementation. Rust's inline mod blocks keep one file idiomatic to roughly 2,500 lines; at 5,500 it becomes a stunt that costs you inside the 25% Code Quality criterion to buy points in a bonus.
Scale ambiguity, flagged. The rubric produces a weighted average on a 5-point scale and the bonus table awards up to +5. Those units cannot both be literal — a +5 bonus would outweigh an entire project. Plan for the conservative reading, claim everything cheap anyway, because under the aggressive reading the three free bonuses are decisive.
07

Honest counterarguments

  • A photo gallery sounds less serious than a typesetting engine. Until the judge learns you wrote the JPEG decoder. The demo must make that explicit — the "lift the lid" beat at 2:40 exists precisely to convert a friendly product into an obvious feat.
  • Baseline JPEG decode is the whole project's crux. True. It is roughly 900–1,200 fiddly lines and it gates rungs 2 through 4. Mitigation is that rung 1 ships without it, and that progressive JPEG is explicitly out of scope and declared as such.
  • Naive decoding will be slow on a big library. Yes, and slow is fine if it's honest. Parallelise with a hand-rolled std::thread worker pool — which is itself the substitution that replaces rayon — cache thumbnails on disk, and publish the throughput number rather than hiding it.
  • Five subsystems risk sprawl. The real threat to the 25% criterion. Enforce clean module seams from hour one: jpeg, png, exif, phash, qr, http, index. Each independently testable, none reaching into another.
  • zdrop is the safer version of this demo. Correct, and it is also the fallback — darkroom degrades into it rather than competing with it.
